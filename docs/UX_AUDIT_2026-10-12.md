# Vasbyt usability and visual consistency audit

Date: 12 October 2026. Focus: older users, clear registration and account ownership, consistent design, useful media, and understandable administration.

## Main findings

The site already has a recognisable identity: event photography, indigo and orange, warm backgrounds, large headings, rounded controls, and a shared header. Public information pages are substantially more media-rich than login, account creation, the shop, and the transaction screens. The best next step is to improve what the interface explains and use media to support decisions.

The highest-priority changes are:

1. Explain whose account is being created and how that person identifies themselves among the participants. Account creation from an older receipt can currently prefill a newer order's buyer details.
2. Preserve participant drafts across refreshes and explain what is saved. Tab switching preserves drafts, but refresh loses them.
3. Make participant validation actionable and put readable terms and the waiver beside the consent checkbox.
4. Simplify product creation, particularly the transition from a product to its variants and the separate Save buttons.
5. Reconcile event times across the programme, route pages, and supporting copy.
6. Correct white text on orange buttons. The measured combination is approximately 3.50:1, below the 4.5:1 requirement for the sampled normal-sized text.

These are expert-review findings and usability hypotheses, not findings from sessions with older participants. Age alone does not determine digital ability. The review concentrates on possible barriers involving vision, dexterity, attention, and remembering steps. W3C explains the overlap between older users' needs and accessibility requirements. [Older users and accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/older-users/).

## Method, environment, and limits

The browser walkthrough used the current Angular development frontend at `http://127.0.0.1:4200`, with the existing local demo API at port 5080 and the separate `vasbyt_demo` database. The frontend development compilation succeeded. The API used the existing compiled local build. Live payment-provider credentials were disabled, and demo mode suppresses registration email. This is an audit of the local working tree and demonstration data, not a production deployment audit.

Screens were inspected using computer use, screenshots, accessibility trees, and read-only DOM observations. Source was read to inventory routes and confirm behavior that was unclear in the interface. Research used W3C WAI/WCAG and the GOV.UK Design System. Browser findings were checked directly; source-only risks are labelled separately below. A source inventory and research pass supplemented the walkthrough, but no delegated browser finding is presented as verified evidence.

Representative responsive checks used a 390-pixel viewport, with approximately 375 pixels of content width after the desktop browser scrollbar, and a 320-pixel viewport for account-form reflow. This does not constitute testing every page at every breakpoint. Full browser zoom, physical phone cameras, screen-reader operation, and a complete keyboard-only walkthrough remain to be tested.

No application source, styling, configuration, Git state, or project-management records were changed. Existing uncommitted work was preserved. This report and its screenshot folder are the audit deliverables.

## Priorities and evidence labels

- **P1:** address first, because the issue can mislead identity or consent decisions, lose substantial form work, affect event attendance, or create a demonstrated accessibility barrier.
- **P2:** address in the next usability pass, because the issue adds uncertainty, effort, or avoidable navigation.
- **P3:** visual or content refinement after the major journeys are clear.

**Observed** means reproduced or read in the browser. **Source-supported** means corroborated by code. **Source-only** means not executed in the browser. Proposed designs and copy are recommendations to validate with users, not observed defects or requirements imposed by a research source.

## Account creation and selecting yourself

### A1. P1: Account ownership and participant identity are not clearly connected

**Observed:** checkout distinguishes buying as a guest from signing in. Account creation uses First name, Surname, Email, and Password. Participant forms later offer I am this participant. There is no explicit account-owner summary or durable indication of which participant is the account holder.

**Source-supported:** the account owns orders, and an order can contain several participants. The participant button copies the order's buyer contact details, rather than establishing a participant-to-account association. See [register-entrant.ts](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/src/Vasbyt.Frontend/src/app/pages/register-entrant.ts:399) and [account.ts](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/src/Vasbyt.Frontend/src/app/pages/account.ts:23).

**Why this matters:** someone registering a spouse, children, or a club can reasonably wonder whether they need one login per participant, whose email/password belongs here, and whether selecting themselves changes the account owner. A buyer who is not participating also needs a clear path.

**Recommendation:** explicitly separate three roles: account owner, buyer/contact person, and participants. State that one person's account can manage the order and its participant passes. Ask whether the account owner is participating, with an explicit I am only registering other people option. Do not silently create accounts for the other participants or infer identity from a shared surname/email.

**Suggested English copy:**

> Create your Vasbyt account. This account belongs to you and lets you manage this order and the people you are registering. Other participants do not need their own accounts to be included in this order.

> Are you taking part? Choose “I am participating” or “I am registering other people only”.

**Suggested Afrikaans copy:**

> Skep jou Vasbyt-rekening. Hierdie rekening behoort aan jou en help jou om die bestelling en die mense wat jy inskryf te bestuur. Ander deelnemers het nie hul eie rekeninge nodig om by hierdie bestelling ingesluit te word nie.

> Neem jy self deel? Kies “Ek neem self deel” of “Ek skryf net ander mense in”.

Names of participants do not exist yet when the current checkout buys quantities. A person selector should therefore appear when participant names are available, or ask the buyer which paid ticket is theirs when forms begin. Do not offer a misleading list of unnamed people during early account creation.

### A2. P1: An old receipt can prefill account creation from a newer buyer

**Reproduction:** complete guest order 000017, start shop order 000018 with different synthetic buyer details, reopen 000017's receipt, then select Create account. The form displayed the shop order's buyer, UX Audit Shop, instead of the receipt's buyer, UX Audit Test.

Immediately after the original paid checkout, the same account entry point had blank identity fields. Both behaviors follow from [create-account.ts](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/src/Vasbyt.Frontend/src/app/pages/create-account.ts:70), which prefills from the current cart, not the receipt being viewed.

**Recommendation:** preserve explicit account-creation context. A checkout entry point may use that checkout's buyer details. A receipt entry point should explain the order reference and ask who will own the account, with the receipt's buyer offered deliberately. Never silently borrow identity details from an unrelated newer basket. Account ownership can legitimately differ from buyer identity, but that must be an explicit choice.

![Account creation from an older receipt uses the newer order's buyer](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/docs/ux-audit-2026-10-12/account-from-old-receipt-uses-new-buyer.jpg)

### A3. P2: Return copy and recovery choices are too generic

**Observed:** both checkout and receipt account creation say You come back to where you were afterwards. Your basket is kept. On a paid receipt the relevant task is finding/linking an order, and the basket may already be empty or belong to another order. There is no prominent Continue as guest or Back to order action on account creation itself.

**Recommendation:** use context-specific actions: Create account and return to checkout; Create account and return to order VB-2027-000017; Continue as guest; Back to order. On the receipt show the chosen account's name/email beside the explicit link action. Make its scope clear: Link this entire order and its participant passes to this account. Avoid implying that account creation has already linked the order.

GOV.UK recommends optional accounts where possible, clear separation of sign-in from account creation, and avoiding repeat data entry. These principles support keeping the existing guest route. [Create accounts](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/create-accounts/).

### A4. P1: Account validation refers to marked fields without useful field errors

**Observed:** submitting an empty account form produced Please check the marked fields. The untouched empty fields had no specific visible error text. A missing password in the prefilled form produced the same generic alert. The password field also has no Show password control.

**Recommendation:** show specific field errors and a linked error summary, move focus to that summary or the first error, and preserve input. Use Create a password as the creation label, with a Show/Hide control. Keep password-manager support, autocomplete, and paste. The source already includes appropriate autocomplete tokens, which should be retained.

WCAG accessible authentication addresses cognitive-test barriers and support mechanisms; it does not mandate a particular signup layout. [Accessible authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html). A conventional reveal control is demonstrated by [GOV.UK password input](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/password-input/).

### A5. P2: Recovery errors can change language and give no direct next action

**Observed:** English password recovery returned the Afrikaans message that recovery was unavailable and the user should contact organisers. The form had initially promised a recovery email. Demo recovery is intentionally disabled, so the absence of delivery is expected; the language and recovery guidance still deserve attention.

**Recommendation:** show availability before collecting an email when known, translate error codes into the selected language, and provide a direct organiser contact link. Test the configured production email path separately. The incomplete reset-link screen already gives a sensible request-a-new-link action.

## Registration and participant forms

### R1. P1: Refresh loses an unfinished participant draft

**Observed:** buyer-copy details and a synthetic town remained when switching participant tabs. Language switching also preserved them. In a separate controlled check, Synthetic draft check was entered as the first name, recorded in a screenshot, and the page refreshed. The field returned blank without a useful recovery message.

**Source-supported:** [order-flow.service.ts](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/src/Vasbyt.Frontend/src/app/core/order-flow.service.ts:53) keeps participant drafts in memory.

**Recommendation:** distinguish Draft saved from Participant submitted. Prefer a resumable draft mechanism that can safely handle the sensitive fields; do not indiscriminately store identity and medical data in persistent browser storage. Provide an unsaved-work warning until durable saving is implemented. Explain what survives a refresh and when the user may return.

### R2. P1: A disabled Save button gives little help completing a long form

**Observed:** Save entrant stays disabled while the form is incomplete. The page contains personal details, address, medical information, emergency contact, and consents. Only some optional fields say Optional. Medication and medical-scheme fields look similar to required fields, despite being optional in the DOM.

**Recommendation:** clearly mark optional fields, explain required groups before entry, and let Save identify missing fields with a focused error summary. Consider a small number of coherent sections with progress and a review step rather than making users inspect the entire page to discover why Save is unavailable. Retain tab drafts and the intentional address-reuse feature.

A summary should link directly to each erroneous field, match the inline wording, and preserve entered values. [GOV.UK error summary](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/). Focused question groups and asking only necessary information reduce form burden. [Question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/).

### R3. P1: Consent has no visible terms or waiver link at the point of agreement

**Observed:** the checkbox says I accept the terms and conditions and the indemnity. Neither the checkbox row nor its surrounding consent section offers a readable terms/indemnity link. General event conditions exist elsewhere on the programme page, but the user is not directed there and the actual waiver text is not presented beside consent.

**Recommendation:** add distinct Read the terms and Read the indemnity links, preserve the form when opening them, and make acceptance separate from optional marketing-photo permission. Obtain organiser confirmation that the displayed documents are the versions intended for agreement. This is a usability finding about access to the text, not a legal determination.

### R4. P2: The early selection screen does not explain the full journey

**Observed:** Choose your items introduces route quantities. The explanation that names/details are entered after payment appears later in the basket and checkout. Products are an optional screen, but the step rail remains on Entries. On mobile, only the current step label is shown beside otherwise unexplained numbers.

**Recommendation:** say up front: Choose how many people you are entering. Add optional products. Pay once. Then complete one form per person. List information needed before payment so a family buyer can prepare. Keep route and tariff quantities on one screen, as the current product requirements specify. Clarify that payment secures the purchase and completed participant forms are still required.

Show a readable Step 1 of 6 label on phones, and name the optional shop step in the surrounding content. A persistent mobile summary can show entry count, total, and Continue without covering fields.

![Registration at phone width](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/docs/ux-audit-2026-10-12/registration-mobile.jpg)

### R5. P2: Ticket labels change as the user moves through checkout

**Observed:** a ticket is Standard/Normaal in selection, becomes a long Afrikaans early-entry date label in an English basket, then appears as Standard again on the participant form. The basket label does not consistently retain the explicit tariff-group name. Route distances also vary in precision between home, route cards, and registration.

**Recommendation:** keep the ticket identity consistent everywhere: Ligstap, Walking, Standard, Early entry, quantity, unit price. Localise the price-window description or use separate translated structured labels. Choose one intentional distance display convention and explicitly say whether a number is the three-day total or one day's distance.

### R6. P2: Shirt size appears mandatory independently of a shirt purchase

**Observed:** the participant form requires Shirt size even when the selected M merchandise shirt already has a size. The form does not explain whether this size is for included kit, a second shirt, or a planning requirement.

**Recommendation:** confirm the actual event policy, then explain it beside the field. If an entry includes a shirt, state that and distinguish it from separately purchased merchandise. If not necessary for every participant, make it conditional or optional. Do not remove it solely on the basis of this audit.

### R7. P2: Paid and fully registered states need a stronger distinction

**Observed:** the receipt heading says Your entry has been received and the rail is on Done while two forms remain open. The What to do next section does list them correctly, but it comes after the prominent success heading and payment summary.

**Recommendation:** lead with Payment received, 2 participant forms still to complete. Give Finish participant forms one primary action, list each person's status, and show Registration complete only when the required forms are complete. Keep a product-only receipt free of participant language. Add a named organiser-help action to the payment/form screens.

The existing reference numbers, private resume links, clear totals, and separate collection status are useful foundations. A final review with specific Change links helps users assess their choices. [Check answers](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/check-answers/).

## Product, programme, and content administration

### D1. P1: Variant setup has an unexplained save-first dependency

**Observed reproduction:** Products, New product, turn on Different variants. Price and stock fields disappear, but no variant rows or New variant control appear. Only Save remains. The editor requires the product to be saved before variant editing becomes available.

**Recommendation:** make this a single understandable creation task. Ask Does this product have options such as sizes?, allow the choices and prices to be entered before the first final save, and show a buyer-facing preview. If the backend dependency remains, make the next action explicit: Save product and add sizes, followed by a clearly labelled second step. Do not silently publish an incomplete product with an Active default.

![New product with variants enabled before its first save](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/docs/ux-audit-2026-10-12/product-variants-before-save.jpg)

### D2. P1: Product and row Save actions have different scopes

**Observed:** each product has Save, while each variant has its own Save. The interface does not prominently distinguish saving product copy from saving prices/stock. Several repeated controls have generic labels or no accessible names beyond the displayed value in the DOM snapshot.

**Source-only risk:** successful save reloads the product list, so other unsaved product/variant drafts may be replaced. This was not reproduced by publishing admin edits. See [admin-products.ts](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/src/Vasbyt.Frontend/src/app/pages/admin/admin-products.ts:307).

**Recommendation:** offer Save all product changes, or explicitly name separate actions such as Save product details and Save M size. Show Changed, Saving, and Saved on the affected object. Give every input and action an accessible name including its product/variant context. Preserve unsaved work during refresh after a save.

### D3. P2: Mobile variants hide important controls in a wide table

**Observed:** at the 390-pixel viewport the variant table was approximately 852 pixels wide inside a 295-pixel container. Price was visible, but stock, Active, and Save required horizontal scrolling. A scrollbar exists, but the editor loses the relationship between a size name and its offscreen actions.

**Recommendation:** use stacked variant cards on phones, with size, price, stock mode, availability, and Save together. On desktop, retain a compact table if it is clearly labelled. A genuinely tabular layout may qualify for WCAG's reflow exception, so this measurement alone is not a blanket conformance failure. It is a significant usability issue for this editing task. [Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html).

![Product variants require horizontal scrolling on a phone](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/docs/ux-audit-2026-10-12/products-mobile.jpg)

### D4. P2: Disabling variant mode is described as deleting variants

**Observed:** Different variants is disabled for the multi-size shirt and says Delete all variants but one to turn this off. This frames deletion as the normal way to change a selling configuration.

**Recommendation:** offer a deliberate conversion flow: select the option to keep, explain what will happen to the others and their history, and preview the resulting price/stock. Make archive/deactivate distinct from permanent deletion. Name the affected product or size in confirmation text. Actual deletion behavior was source-reviewed, not executed.

### D5. P2: Adverts hides two different content tasks behind one label

**Observed:** the Adverts tab leads to Accommodation and sponsors, with Both, Accommodation, Sponsor, and New advert. A new blank advert defaults to Accommodation. Booking link and phone fields also appear in the shared editor. New advert requires an image before Save becomes available, and this requirement is explained.

**Recommendation:** rename the navigation to Accommodation and sponsors, or give the user separate Add accommodation and Add sponsor actions. Pick the type before showing fields. Use Display order instead of Order, Website address instead of Link, and show booking-specific fields only where relevant. Preserve the useful visible image-required explanation.

### D6. P1: New tariff rules start with a zero-duration window

**Observed:** New tariff rule prefills Valid from and Valid to with the same current timestamp, amount zero, and Active checked. Existing rules use date/time values with little explanation of boundaries or overlap. The warning says Nothing caches this table, which describes implementation rather than the organiser's task.

**Recommendation:** require an intentional end date/time, validate it after the start, explain inclusive/exclusive boundaries in plain language, and flag overlaps before publication. Show a concise impact preview, for example Standard entry becomes R560 on 1 March at 02:00 South African time. Use New entry price instead of tariff jargon if organisers understand it better. Keep the current buyer-price preview and rewrite the warning to Changes apply to new purchases immediately.

Saving these defaults was not executed, so no claim is made that the server accepts them. See [admin-tariffs.ts](/home/stephanbredenhann/Documents/GitHub/vasbyt-projek/src/Vasbyt.Frontend/src/app/pages/admin/admin-tariffs.ts:201).

### D7. P2: Shared walking-route fields look unavailable without a direct next action

**Observed:** Ligstap/Vasstap have disabled distance, climb, and grade fields, no day editor, and This route uses the Ligdraf/Vasbyt days. Edit them there. There is no direct link in that sentence. Introductory copy also implies walking routes get their real distances here, which conflicts with editing the source running route.

**Recommendation:** show Inherited from Ligdraf and an Edit shared route details button that moves to that editor. Explain which fields are independent, which changes affect both categories, and keep the inheritance relationship visible during editing.

### D8. P2: Programme editing is a long repeated bilingual form

**Observed:** all three days and every activity expose paired Afrikaans/English titles/details, time, move controls, and removal. The save bar is helpful, but finding one activity involves substantial scrolling and repeated field labels.

**Recommendation:** use day navigation with activity summaries, expand one activity to edit, retain paired-language editing, and show translation completeness. Keep a persistent named save action and the existing unsaved-change protection. Show that programme and route-day schedules are separate or, preferably, derive their common time/date values from the same event schedule.

### D9. P2: Admin search conventions and unnamed participants need clearer signals

**Observed/source-supported:** Orders has a Search button; Participants uses a field change event. These are different search conventions. Blank participants have duplicate Form still blank rows, and the details editor can display `0001-01-01` for an unprovided date of birth.

**Recommendation:** use one explicit search convention in both screens, show which filters are applied, and name blanks Participant 1/2 with their order and route. Show Not provided instead of a technical default date. Keep payment and form-completion statuses separate. Avoid exposing implementation codes as the principal status cue.

### D10. P2: Reception mixes account, order, arrival, and collection terminology

**Observed:** manual pass lookup worked and the result offers Record arrival and product collection separately. A person selector says Others on this account, even though the source retrieves people from the same order; an unfinished person's option can appear as / Ligtrap (form incomplete).

**Recommendation:** say Other participants in this order, use Participant 2 when a name is missing, and group arrival recording separately from product handover. Make incomplete-registration status clear before staff decide what to do. Retain manual entry and photo alternatives to the camera, plus the visible arrival status.

### D11. P2: Image cropping needs alternatives to dragging

**Source-only:** the cropper uses pointer dragging to pan and a slider to zoom. The source does not provide a keyboard pan control or an obvious non-drag alternative for positioning. Actual upload/crop operation was not executed.

**Recommendation:** add directional controls, reset/centre, zoom buttons, and a clearly explained crop preview. Distinguish Image uploaded from Product saved. Keep the displayed JPEG/PNG/WebP and 5 MB limits. Announce upload errors and preserve the draft. [Dragging movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html), [File upload](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/file-upload/).

### D12. P2: Administrator promotion and invitation share one action

**Observed:** the screen explains that an existing account is promoted immediately, while a new address receives an invitation. The button says Make administrator for both.

**Recommendation:** review the email and identify the outcome before execution: Grant administrator access to an existing account, or Send administrator invitation. Explain the access scope and display the result clearly. No privileges or invitations were changed during this audit.

## Public pages, consistency, and useful media

### V1. P1: Sampled orange actions do not meet normal-text contrast

**Measured:** the header Enter now button renders white `rgb(255,255,255)` text on `rgb(241,83,28)`, equivalent to `#f1531c`. Text was 16 pixels, weight 600. The hero action was 17 pixels. Relative-luminance calculation gives approximately **3.50:1**.

**Recommendation:** use dark text on the brand orange or an adequately darkened orange behind white text. Verify normal, hover, focus, disabled, and selected states. Do not rely on the dark border to improve the text/background ratio. Similar white/orange status chips deserve checking.

WCAG's minimum is 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text; the sampled button text is normal-sized. This is a demonstrated issue for those controls, not a claim that every site color fails. [Contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

### V2. P1: Event times disagree across visible information

**Observed in demo content:**

- Programme Thursday Start: 18:15. Route-detail Day 1 start: 18:00. Dorpsdraf supporting copy also says 18:00.
- Programme Saturday Start: 06:30. Vastrap and Ligdraf/Ligstap show 06:00 for Day 3.
- Friday schedule says fires are lit at 17:00; the bring-and-braai paragraph says welcome from 17:00 and fire at 18:00.

Route-specific exceptions can be legitimate, but the generic Start rows do not explain them. With strict-time wording, a visitor cannot know which time to follow.

**Recommendation:** obtain organiser confirmation, show route-specific exceptions directly in the programme, and publish one consistent set of times. Add explicit calendar dates to every route-day heading, not just Day 1, Thursday. Distinguish briefing, gathering, transport departure, and race start. This report does not decide the correct event times.

### V3. P2: Home has a countdown but omits a stable date/location summary beside the main CTA

**Observed:** the loaded hero shows the event name, tagline, registration/calendar actions, and countdown. The actual date range is easy to find on the programme or receipt, but is not displayed plainly beside the homepage's primary registration action.

**Recommendation:** show 29 April to 1 May 2027, Orania, Northern Cape, subject to the organiser's confirmed source, beside the CTA. Add three short discipline labels. Keep the countdown secondary. Do not flash provisional/empty timing copy while configuration is still loading.

### V4. P2: Homepage route summaries are not clear paths into route details

**Observed:** home route summaries have names, distances, difficulty and counts, but no explicit View route link in the loaded accessibility/DOM snapshot. The Routes page does have these links. Its four side-by-side cards contain long prose and put detailed decision help lower down.

**Recommendation:** give each home category an explicit View route action. Show total distance over three days, discipline, difficulty, and a concise practical description before the longer story. Keep the running/walking explanation, which is already useful. Add a short comparison view or optional filters; avoid increasing the card density with more decorative badges.

### V5. P2: Media in the shop and accommodation does not answer the visitor's main question

**Observed:** the shirt uses the event logo rather than a photo of the shirt; the supporters' pack uses a group event photograph and does not explain its contents in the card. The demo accommodation entry uses a family/community photo, has no price/location/availability detail, and no booking/contact action. Sponsor content is also seeded sample material.

**Recommendation:** replace sample commercial media with organiser-approved images. Show the actual shirt, front/back/detail views and a size guide; show what the pack contains. Accommodation should show the property, rooms/campsite, location, accessibility features, approximate distance to registration, and how to check availability. Use sponsor logos as identification, with separate event photography for the story.

These are demo-content gaps, not proof that a confirmed production listing is broken. Richness should mean better evidence for a decision, not simply more image area.

### V6. P2: Login is visually sparse compared with the public story pages

**Observed:** desktop login is a small left-aligned form with substantial unused space to the right. Account creation has a similar composition. The shared colors/type are consistent, but the transition from photo-rich public pages is abrupt.

**Recommendation:** add one calm, authentic event/community photo beside the form on desktop, with a concise account-benefit sentence. Give the form a readable solid background, keep ownership context near its fields, and move the image below or omit it on small screens. The image must not push the primary action below a long decorative hero. Use the same approach on account creation; keep payment and medical entry focused.

### V7. P2: Gallery autoplay has no visible persistent pause control

**Observed/source-supported:** the gallery provides Previous/Next controls, and source advances it automatically every six seconds. It pauses while hovered/focused and respects reduced motion, but no persistent Pause/Play action is rendered.

**Recommendation:** add an explicit Pause/Play control or make the gallery manual by default. Keep photography and reduced-motion support. WCAG explains that a pause tied only to holding focus does not provide an independent pause mechanism. [Pause, stop, hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html).

### V8. P2: English is incomplete across stored content and supporting labels

**Observed:** English screens retain Afrikaans product/advert descriptions and order-line labels. Route-detail difficulty chips show Matig/Swaar where the listing shows Moderate/Hard. Province options and some accessible navigation/map descriptions remain Afrikaans. The skip link is Spring na inhoud in English mode.

**Recommendation:** keep the visible language selector in a predictable location, consider full English/Afrikaans names, and translate supporting/accessibility labels as well as headings. Provide bilingual fields for managed content that requires both versions, and flag missing translations. Proper names can remain unchanged. The tested root document language was English after reload, so a suspected root-language initialization issue was not confirmed and is not included as a defect.

Repeated navigation already stays in a consistent relative order, which should be retained. [Consistent navigation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/consistent-navigation.html).

### V9. P2: Missing/error states do not always offer a recovery action

**Observed:** an invalid route shows only This route does not exist below the header. It lacks an in-content All routes or contact action. Recovery errors also often depend on users finding another navigation link themselves.

**Source-only:** accommodation and sponsor fetch failures are converted to the same empty-list state as no published content. Several admin lists initially render empty/closed wording before responses arrive, which was briefly visible during the walkthrough.

**Recommendation:** distinguish Loading, No published content, and Could not load. Offer Retry and an appropriate next action. For an unknown route provide View all routes. Preserve cart/form state on recoverable errors. Do not present a transient absence of response as entries being closed.

### V10. P3: Important FAQ answers and help links should be brought into the relevant tasks

**Observed:** FAQ states that age limits and student/scholar proof are still being confirmed while registration offers those categories. FAQ, About, and Sponsors are reached mainly through the footer. The programme contains extensive useful preparation information below the schedule.

**Recommendation:** resolve eligibility policy before taking affected entries, and surface it beside the tariff/category choices. Link registration help from checkout/forms, What to bring from route detail/receipt, and accommodation from preparation guidance. Group FAQ topics and provide visible jump links. Keep the accessible native disclosure behavior.

The emergency contact shown on About should also be confirmed by the organiser: the source contains a note that supplied documents disagree. This is a content-owner follow-up, not a recommendation to replace it with an unverified number.

## Proposed easier journeys

### Buyer account and multi-person registration

1. Choose ticket quantities with a plain explanation of the full journey and eligibility rules.
2. Add optional products and a donation, then review clearly named route/tariff lines.
3. Enter buyer/contact details. Offer guest checkout as a normal visible choice.
4. If creating an account, show the owner's name/email explicitly and explain that it can manage all participants in the order. Preserve checkout context and data.
5. Ask whether the owner/buyer participates. Allow a clear Not participating answer.
6. Review and pay through the intended production provider, with an accurate resume/status explanation.
7. Present a list of paid tickets with participant status. Choose which ticket is mine, or which named participant is me once names exist. Show name plus route/tariff and allow correcting this selection without changing another person's details silently.
8. Complete short coherent form sections, save drafts safely, review them, then submit consent against readable documents.
9. Show Payment received and Forms complete as separate statuses. Provide the receipt, passes, collection instructions, and explicit account-link action where needed.

For a nonparticipating parent/club organiser, the account remains theirs while all participants are marked Other person. A participant wanting an independent account later needs an explicit invitation/ownership design. That is a product decision, not something the current buyer-copy button already implements.

### Product creation

1. Add product: name, description, display order, and draft visibility.
2. Upload actual product photos, with clear crop controls and preview.
3. Choose One price or Options such as sizes.
4. Enter prices and stock behavior in the same task, using plain Unlimited stock versus Track available quantity wording.
5. Preview the buyer-facing card/dialog, including unavailable options.
6. Review the product, options, image, and publication state.
7. Save all changes with an explicit confirmation, retaining other unsaved drafts.

### Programme and route setup

Start from three named days, each with date, briefing, transport, and category-specific start times. Edit one day/activity at a time. Show how shared running/walking schedules relate. Translate paired copy in context, preview the public view, then publish with a visible summary of changed times. Validate contradictions rather than expecting organisers to remember which separate editor controls each value.

## Media and consistency direction

Retain the current Vasbyt identity. A new generic palette or font system is unnecessary for these findings.

- **Home:** current hero and gallery, with stable date/location information, short captions, and explicit route links.
- **Routes:** authentic discipline-specific images, readable maps, named total/per-day distances, and logistics that are visible without deciphering GPX terminology.
- **Programme:** retain the new photo hero and activity photos; make the schedule the practical focal point. Add preparation jump links or a simple printable view later.
- **Accommodation:** actual property evidence and booking details take precedence over a decorative banner.
- **Sponsors:** legible genuine logos and sponsorship context.
- **About/donations:** current human photography works well. Add verified impact information if available, without inventing statistics.
- **Login/account creation:** one supporting photo and clear task/account benefits.
- **Shop:** actual merchandise imagery, contents, sizing, collection information, and a consistent image treatment.
- **Checkout/medical forms/admin:** use small purposeful thumbnails/previews only where they help verify a choice. Avoid large moving media during concentrated data entry.

Use consistent heading spacing, card padding, button roles, and labels. Indigo can denote the primary task action; orange can remain a brand accent with accessible text. Do not make unrelated tasks appear selected just because they share the same bright style. Keep display typography for short headings and the body font for labels, prose, references, and data.

If video is added, use a still poster, user-initiated playback, captions, and an accompanying text summary. Reserve image space, optimise formats and dimensions, and load below-the-fold media lazily. Do not measure media richness by the number of autoplay elements.

## Accessibility targets and validation

Distinguish conformance floors from this project's preferred usability targets:

- Normal text contrast: at least 4.5:1. Large text: at least 3:1. [Contrast minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).
- WCAG 2.2 AA pointer target minimum: 24 by 24 CSS pixels or an applicable exception. Prefer 44 to 48 pixels for frequent standalone controls in this audience; this larger preference is not the AA minimum. [Target size minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).
- Resize text to 200% without losing content/function, and assess reflow at an equivalent 320 CSS pixels. A narrow viewport check alone does not verify browser zoom. [Resize text](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html), [Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html).
- Preserve visible keyboard focus, logical navigation, explicit input names, understandable errors, and alternatives to drag gestures.
- Retain 17 to 18 pixel body text where currently used. There is no universal WCAG minimum font size; test readability with users rather than presenting a chosen body size as a compliance rule.

### Acceptance checks for the proposed improvements

- A buyer can explain who owns the account, whether they are participating, which participant is themselves, and which passes the account manages.
- Creating an account from an old receipt never silently uses a newer basket's identity.
- Refreshing an unfinished participant form either restores its draft or gives a truthful warning before loss.
- A participant can discover every missing field and reach it from the error summary.
- Terms and indemnity text can be read without losing entered form data.
- Shirt-size requirements and student/scholar eligibility are explained before commitment.
- A new multi-size product can be completed without an unexplained Save-first handoff.
- Product Save scope is explicit, and one save does not discard other unsaved edits.
- Every variant's price/stock/actions remain identifiable at phone width and through accessible names.
- Programme, route starts, transport, supporting paragraphs, and calendar information agree or clearly explain exceptions.
- Sampled action text meets its contrast requirement in all relevant states.
- Empty, loading, unavailable, and error states are visually and verbally distinct.
- Both languages preserve typed data and provide equivalent instructions and actionable errors.

Run usability sessions with older Afrikaans- and English-speaking users of differing digital confidence. Ask them to register themselves plus another person, register others without participating, create/retrieve an account, buy a shirt in a chosen size, resume an interrupted order, find accommodation, and determine their start/transport time. For organisers, test adding a multi-size product and updating one activity without losing another draft. Observe help requests, wrong-owner choices, missed fields, accidental actions, backtracking, and completion independently of hints. A small first round can expose problems; it cannot establish population-wide success rates.

## Suggested implementation order

1. Account ownership/context and participant self-selection; draft loss, consent access, validation, and confirmed event times.
2. Measured action contrast and named admin inputs; product creation/save scope and mobile variants.
3. Bilingual stored content/errors, clearer tariff/route dependencies, loading/recovery states, and task-specific help.
4. Purposeful media on login and shop, genuine accommodation/product content, gallery pause, and public-page refinements.
5. Recheck all affected journeys with older users and assistive technology, then complete the production payment/email/camera and final submission branches that were not executed here.
